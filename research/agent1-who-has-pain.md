# Redline Research — Agent 1: WHO HAS THIS PAIN?

## 1. Summary
People across freelance, employment, gym/subscription, and small-business vendor contracts do get hurt by terms they missed or misjudged — cancellation fees, non-competes, missing late-fee clauses, IP assignment, deceptive auto-charge language. The strongest, best-documented case is small businesses tricked by payment-processor cancellation-fee clauses they weren't told about (FTC action, real dollar figures). Evidence is weaker and thinner for residential leases and SaaS/ToS specifically — I could not find a strong, individually-sourced "I signed a lease/ToS and a clause I didn't understand cost me $X" story within budget. The evidence does NOT show that people would pay for a tool like Redline, that "didn't understand the clause" (vs. "had no leverage to negotiate" or "trusted the other party") was the actual root cause in most cases, or that reading a plain-English summary would have changed the outcome.

## 4. SYNTHETIC PERSONAS (constructed, not real people)

**Persona A — "Maria, the solo contractor"**
- Freelancer/solo contractor who takes on client work without a lawyer on retainer [F2, F3, F4]
- Signs contracts (or works without one) based on trust in the client relationship, not legal review [F4]
- Gets hurt by omissions in her own contract as much as by hostile clauses in someone else's — e.g., missing late-fee terms, informal equity promises never made contractual [F2, F3]
- Damage shows up as unpaid/delayed invoices and lost equity, not a single catastrophic clause [F2, F3, F4]
- Reaction afterward is regret and a resolution to change her own contract template going forward, not litigation [F3]
- Thin evidence note: none of F2–F4 give her age, location, or niche — occupation/business-size detail is largely "not stated" in the source.

**Persona B — "Small business owner sold a service contract in a language/format they can't fully vet"**
- Runs a small business (e.g. a shop or restaurant) approached by a vendor's sales rep with verbal promises that contradict the written agreement [F1]
- Operates with limited English proficiency; contract paperwork is only in the dominant language, not the language sales was conducted in [F1]
- Bitten by a specific clause (multi-year term + fixed cancellation fee) that was never disclosed accurately at the point of sale [F1]
- Damage is concrete and dollar-denominated: a $495 cancellation fee, continued unauthorized withdrawals after canceling [F1]
- Recourse came from a regulator (FTC), not from the business owner catching the clause themselves — suggesting self-service contract review tools would need to reach this person before signing, not after [F1]
- Thin evidence note: this persona rests on one source (F1); no individual first-person quotes, only the regulatory complaint's aggregated language.

**Persona C — "Early employee blindsided by a non-compete's real-world weight"**
- Joined a startup as a rank-and-file employee (not a senior hire with legal counsel) [F5]
- Signed a non-compete as a condition of employment without a clear sense of when/whether it would bind them if the job ended [F5]
- Trigger event was an involuntary job loss (furlough, no severance), at which point the non-compete's implications suddenly mattered [F5]
- Sought answers from an anonymous peer community rather than a lawyer, suggesting cost/access is a barrier to getting a real legal read [F5]
- Thin evidence note: weakest of the three personas — built from a single forum post (F5); we don't know if they ever got clarity, what their outcome was, or basic demographic detail beyond city (Pittsburgh, PA).

## 5. Signals that contradict or weaken the Redline hypothesis
- In F5, once the poster asked, multiple commenters said the clause was very likely unenforceable regardless of what it said — suggesting the real unmet need may be "tell me if this is enforceable" (a legal-risk judgment), not "summarize what it says," which a plain-English summary alone wouldn't resolve.
- F1 shows the actual failure mode was not that the business owner read the contract and misunderstood a clause — it was that sales reps verbally contradicted the written contract, and the contract was in a language the signer could not fully read. A clause-risk summarizer would help only if it were delivered in the signer's language and if the signer engaged with the tool instead of trusting the verbal sales pitch — the trust/leverage problem, not a comprehension-of-English-legal-text problem, may be primary here.
- F6 shows the damage came from a company designing a cancellation process to be practically unusable (unstaffed cancellation-only location), not from the member failing to read or understand the clause — they clearly knew cancellation required an in-person visit and tried repeatedly. Understanding the clause did not prevent the harm.
- F7 (NYC regulator) frames the fix as disclosure and legal enforcement (a state/city compliance action), not as a consumer-side reading tool — indicating that at least one relevant actor treats this as a market-conduct/regulatory problem, not an individual-comprehension problem.
- F2–F4 (freelancer stories) suggest a lot of freelancer pain came from having NO contract at all, or from omissions (missing clauses), rather than from a hidden risky clause the freelancer failed to notice in an existing contract — this is a different problem from Redline's "flag risky clauses in a document you have" framing.
- I found no case in this research where someone said explicitly "if I had a plain-English summary or risk score before signing, I would have acted differently" — that causal claim is unverified in the sample gathered.

- A strong, individually-sourced SaaS/ToS personal story (a person, not a class-action aggregate) describing being hurt by a ToS clause they didn't read/understand. F9 is a lawsuit allegation, not a first-person account, and I could not verify its wording directly (page blocked).
- Any source with hard numbers on how common this problem is across the whole population (no prevalence data beyond the informal, unsourced "83%" and "68%" statistics that appeared in AI-generated search summaries — I deliberately excluded those as findings because I could not trace them to a primary study).

- Any story where the person explicitly says they would have paid for, or actually used, an AI contract-review tool — no evidence either way on willingness to pay.

### Judgment calls
- Excluded unsourced statistics ("83% of people sign contracts they don't fully understand," "68% of freelancers have unknowingly signed away IP rights") that appeared inside AI-generated search-engine summaries with no traceable primary source — these read as marketing copy from contract-review-tool vendors, not verified data.
- Kept F9 (Trustpilot) despite a blocked page fetch, but explicitly labeled it "[snippet only, not verified on page]" per the guardrail, and treated it as the weakest finding rather than dropping the SaaS/ToS category entirely.
- Kept F8 (security-deposit lightbulb story) despite it being a better fit for "petty/unfair landlord behavior" than "tenant didn't understand a clause," because a stronger lease finding could not be located within the search budget — flagged this explicitly rather than silently upgrading its fit.
- Treated F7 (NYC.gov enforcement action) as corroboration/context for F6 rather than an independent individual persona data point, since it is aggregate regulatory language, not one person's account.
- Did not count the HN thread (news.ycombinator.com/item?id=4713426) as a standalone finding since it was general forum debate about non-compete enforceability, not a first-person account of being harmed — used only as contradicting-signal context in Section 5.
- Reddit could not be fetched directly in this session (blocked/unavailable via the tools provided); all Reddit content here is mediated through secondary sites (Yahoo, AOL) that quote or summarize the original posts — this is disclosed as a limitation in Section 6, and quotes from these secondary sources are treated as the secondary site's rendering, not verified against the original Reddit post itself.
