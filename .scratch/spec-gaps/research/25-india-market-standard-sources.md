# 25. Public sources that could ground "Market standard" for Indian SME contracts

Researched 2026-10-31. Question: which public, citable sources describe typical or recommended terms for Indian small-business vendor/SaaS agreements, commercial leases and leave-and-licence agreements, and client/services agreements, and which Indian law floors bear on Severity (ADR 0003). Builds on ticket 18 (US) without repeating it.

Markings: **[read]** means I read the primary text (page or PDF text extracted locally). **[snippet]** means I saw only a search summary or secondary commentary, so the claim is unverified. **[unverified-recall]** means it comes from background knowledge and needs checking before anyone cites it. Note: india Code, msme.gov.in and mohua.gov.in returned 403/404 to automated fetches this session, so most statute wording below comes from secondary sources.

## Summary
- **No Indian equivalent of Common Paper or Bonterms was found**: no free, licensed, balanced B2B standard form, and no measured benchmark of SME contract terms. The closest primary forms are government ones: GeM GTC (procurement) and the Maharashtra IGR leave-and-licence template.
- **Leases have practice figures but no public survey.** "3-year lock-in", "15% every 3 years" and "5% a year" show up again and again in deal reports and broker blogs, but I found no JLL/CBRE/Knight Frank/Anarock publication that measures lease terms. Their public reports cover volumes and rents.
- **Indian law floors matter more than in the US pass**: MSMED Act ss.15-16 (45-day cap on payment to micro and small suppliers), Contract Act s.27 (restraint of trade void) and s.74 (stated penalties cut back to reasonable compensation), and registration of leases over one year. They give firmer ground than "typical", but they are legal claims (scope question, as in ticket 18 §D).

## Ticket 18 sources: do they apply to Indian contracts?
- **Common Paper / Bonterms forms (CC BY 4.0)**: written to US law. The clause *structures* (1x-fees cap, mutual IP indemnity, auto-renewal with a notice window, 30-day cure) are found in Indian SaaS contracts too, but I found no evidence that Indian counterparties use or benchmark against these forms. Citing them for an Indian contract would mean "departs from a US balanced form". [read in ticket 18; India fit unverified]
- **Common Paper 2026 benchmark**: drawn from Common Paper platform users, most of them US. No India breakdown. Weak fit.
- **CUAD / LEDGAR**: built from SEC EDGAR, so US public companies. Useful for finding clauses, not for Indian norms. Same limits as ticket 18 §E.
- **NY GOL 5-903, CA SB 1103, SBA**: US law. Do not apply.

## A. Vendor / supplier / SaaS agreements

### A1. MSMED Act 2006, ss.15-24 (payment to micro and small suppliers) [snippet for text; read for DC-MSME summary]
- Statute: https://www.indiacode.nic.in/handle/123456789/2013 (403 to fetch). Official summary read: https://www.dcmsme.gov.in/publications/LegalFramework.pdf (undated DC-MSME note).
- s.15: the buyer pays by the agreed date, which cannot be more than 45 days after acceptance or deemed acceptance. With no written agreement the limit is 15 days [snippet: taxguru, Vinod Kothari https://vinodkothari.com/2024/02/the-big-buzz-on-small-business-payment-delays/]. s.16: compound interest with monthly rests at three times the RBI bank rate, payable "notwithstanding anything contained in any agreement" [the DC-MSME note confirms 3x bank rate and 45 days; the override wording is snippet]. s.18: reference to the state MSE Facilitation Council (MSEFC), decided within 90 days. s.19: a buyer appealing an award must first deposit 75% of it [DC-MSME note, read].
- Related: Income-tax Act 1961 s.43B(h) (Finance Act 2023) disallows a buyer's deduction for dues to MSEs paid late; a Rajya Sabha reply reported in July 2026 says it is now s.37(2)(g) of the Income-tax Act 2025 [snippet: https://taxguru.in/?p=1060588]. MCA Form MSME-1 requires half-yearly reporting of MSE dues outstanding beyond 45 days [snippet].
- Severity use: when the Owner is a Udyam-registered micro or small supplier, a clause giving the buyer more than 45 days goes past a statutory cap (and the interest applies anyway). When the Owner is the buyer, it is a Know-this about exposure. A licence question does not arise: the statute is public law.
- Cite: "MSMED Act 2006, s.15 (India Code)".

### A2. MSME Samadhaan and MSME ODR [read]
- https://samadhaan.msme.gov.in/ : the Ministry of MSME's delayed-payment monitoring portal. Dashboards show 1,09,806 cases (Rs 29,784 crore) filed since 30-10-2017 and 63,606 disposed (read 2026-10-31). The site says new applications now go to https://odr.msme.gov.in. Filing needs Udyam registration.
- What it gives: proof that delayed payment is a common SME harm, plus case counts by state and buyer type. It holds no clause terms, so it is not a Market standard source. A product could link to it as the remedy for a late-paying buyer.

### A3. GeM General Terms and Conditions 4.0, v1.29, dated 25 March 2026 [read: full PDF text extracted]
- Official asset host: https://assets-bg.gem.gov.in/resources/pdf/ (older v1.9/v1.10 there). I read v1.29 from a copy published by IISER Berhampur, which presents it as the current version: https://www.iiserbpr.ac.in/uploads/file_upload/GeM%20Latest%20Guidelines%20Mar%202026.pdf . Confirm the current version on gem.gov.in before citing.
- Clauses: **cl.12** payment of 100% within 10 days of the receipt-cum-acceptance certificate (goods) or the service delivery acceptance certificate (monthly service bills), "unless otherwise specified in STC / ATC". **cl.7** performance security of 3-5% of contract value (GFR range), none for direct purchase. **cl.15** liquidated damages of 0.5% of the delayed value per week, max 5%, or 10% for "inordinate" delay (more than 25% of the delivery period). **cl.18** limitation of liability: no special, incidental or consequential damages; aggregate liability of either party capped at total contract value, excluding third-party IPR claims and judicially determined gross negligence or wilful misconduct. **cl.19** termination for default forfeits the security.
- Fit: Indian government buyer terms that bind sellers, many of them MSEs. They are mutual and mostly balanced, but this is public procurement, not private B2B. Tenders often override them in their ATC: one BHEL tender replaces 10 days with 45/60/90 days [snippet].
- Licence: a government publication with no reuse licence stated. Quoting a clause with attribution is probably fine; copying the whole document is a separate question. Cite: "GeM GTC 4.0 v1.29 (25-03-2026), cl.18".

### A4. Government model RFPs and procurement manuals [snippet]
- MeitY model RFP templates (2017, revised 2018) for e-governance implementation agencies and consultants, including model contract agreements. A Delhi circular describes them (https://ddrs.delhi.gov.in/sites/default/files/generic_multiple_files/circular-modal-rfp-dit-gnctd.pdf). A Delhi PWD copy of the 2018 consulting RFP: https://pwd.delhi.gov.in/sites/default/files/pwd/news/model_rfp_selection_of_consulting_services_2018.pdf . Secondary sources say liability is typically capped at contract value (1x TCV). Clause text not read.
- Department of Expenditure procurement manuals (Goods; Consultancy and Other Services) and GFR 2017: not retrieved. Same caveat as A3: these are government-as-buyer terms.

### A5. Private templates and law-firm material [snippet]
- No Indian bar council, NLSIU, iSPIRT or Startup India model SaaS or service agreement was found. Free Indian templates exist on commercial sites (Certyneo, Wonder.legal, Counselvise, Genie AI), with unknown authorship and licence; do not cite. The EU IP Helpdesk lists an "India service agreement template" (https://intellectual-property-helpdesk.ec.europa.eu/ip-management-and-resources/publications/service-agreement-template-india_en), aimed at EU SMEs contracting in India. Not opened; its licence needs checking. It is the most citable private-sector candidate found.
- Indian law-firm commentary (LawSikho https://lawsikho.com/blog/saas-agreement-india/ , Mondaq/IndiaCorpLaw articles) describes common positions but measures nothing. At most it could support a "commonly negotiated" claim.

## B. Commercial leases and leave-and-licence agreements

### B1. Maharashtra IGR leave-and-licence e-registration format [read]
- https://efilingigr.maharashtra.gov.in/ereg/downloadstuff/LAndL.pdf (the format for the IGR online e-registration service, https://igrmaharashtra.gov.in/Home/eregistration_about).
- Clauses: a fixed term in months; monthly compensation due within the first five days, either flat or stepped in blocks of months (the escalation form); an interest-free deposit, refundable or non-refundable (the amount is left blank, no norm stated); maintenance charges on either party; repairs by the licensee subject to wear and tear; no alterations without written consent; no tenancy, sublet or assignment; **one month's written notice on either side** (cl.9); **double daily compensation for overstay** (cl.10); stamp duty and registration paid by the licensee (cl.11). It invokes s.24 of the Maharashtra Rent Control Act 1999. The schedule is drafted for a residential flat.
- Fit: a state-published baseline in Maharashtra's widely used e-registration format, but residential-shaped and with no lock-in clause. Licence: a government form, none stated. Cite: "Maharashtra IGR leave-and-licence format, cl.9".

### B2. Model Tenancy Act 2021 (MoHUA) [snippet]
- Approved by the Union Cabinet on 2 June 2021 as a **model** for states; it binds nobody until a state enacts it, and it applies prospectively. PIB: https://pib.gov.in/ (release not retrieved); the MoHUA PDF URLs I tried returned 404/403. Secondary summaries: JSA https://www.jsalaw.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/07/JSA-Update-Real-Estate-Model-Tenancy-Act-July-27-2021-external-circulation.pdf , Mondaq https://www.mondaq.com/india/landlord-tenant-leases/1082764/model-tenancy-act-a-brief-overview .
- Per the summaries: it covers residential, commercial and educational premises, not industrial use, hotels or lodging houses; a written agreement is mandatory; the security deposit is capped at **2 months' rent residential and 6 months non-residential** (s.11 per one source); rent revision is by agreement, with written notice before it takes effect (secondary sources say 3 months; unverified). Which states have adopted it (and how far they cover commercial premises) was not confirmed. Tamil Nadu's 2017 Act and Uttar Pradesh's 2021 tenancy ordinance/Act are often named as similar regimes [unverified-recall].
- Severity use: a commercial deposit above 6 months would exceed the model cap. That shows a departure from the model norm, but it is the law only in states that adopted the cap.

### B3. Registration and stamp duty [snippet / unverified-recall]
- Registration Act 1908 s.17(1)(d): leases from year to year, for more than one year, or reserving a yearly rent must be registered. Under s.49 an unregistered document cannot be used as evidence of the transaction (with a proviso for collateral purposes). This is why 11-month agreements are common [snippet: iPleaders https://blog.ipleaders.in/rent-agreement-format-india/ ; Mondaq https://www.mondaq.com/india/landlord-tenant-leases/1149210/lease-or-leave-and-license-dynamics-for-commercial-decisions].
- Maharashtra Rent Control Act 1999 s.55: every leave-and-licence agreement must be registered regardless of term. Several secondary sources say so; one disagrees. The IGR format cl.11 ("This Agreement is to be registered") fits the majority reading [read].
- Stamp duty is a state matter (Indian Stamp Act 1899 Sch. I Art. 35 and state schedules, for example Maharashtra Stamp Act Art. 36A for leave and licence) [unverified-recall]. Rates vary by state, so a product should only note "check state stamp duty", not give a number.

### B4. Property consultants (JLL, CBRE, Knight Frank, Anarock, Cushman, Colliers) [snippet]
- No public report giving measured lease-term norms (lock-in, escalation, deposit months) was found. Their free reports give volumes, rents and vacancy; the detailed data is gated or paid. The nearest:
  - Anarock/Images retail report (2025-26): about 75% of retail leases run 3-7 years and 74% use hybrid revenue-share models [snippet: https://www.constructionworld.in/latest-construction-news/real-estate-news/retail-leasing-hits-4.3-mn-sq-ft-in-h2-2025--anarock-releap-2026/90008].
  - "Standard escalation is 5 percent a year" for malls, from a consultant quoted in BloombergQuint [snippet: https://www.bloombergquint.com/amp/bq-blue-exclusive/its-a-great-time-to-be-a-premium-mall-owner-in-india].
  - Office deal reports repeatedly show **3-year lock-ins** and **15% escalation every 3 years** (Aon in Bengaluru, Table Space in Gurugram, Bank of America in Powai) [snippet: constructionworld.in, angelone.in]. Broker blogs put office deposits at anything from 3-6 months to 6-12 months (Bengaluru advisory: "10 months") [snippet: myndsolution.com, realtyhunting.com]. These are large-occupier office deals, not SME shops, and none comes from a measured survey.
- Fit: enough to say a figure appears often in reported deals. Not enough for "Market standard" without Redline curating and dating each figure.

## C. Client / services agreements (Owner as supplier)
- **MSMED Act** (A1) is the main India-specific protection when the Owner is a micro or small enterprise: anything beyond 45 days to pay, or a clause trying to waive s.16 interest, departs from statute.
- **GeM GTC** (A3) is a public-sector model of balanced service terms: 10-day payment, liability capped at 1x contract value, LD capped at 5-10%.
- **MeitY model consulting RFP** (A4) for consultancy-style engagements.
- No Indian freelancer model contract like NYC's was found.

## D. Indian law floors that affect Severity [snippet unless marked]
- **Contract Act s.23**: an agreement whose object or consideration is unlawful or opposed to public policy is void. The Supreme Court has used this, with unequal bargaining power, against one-sided standard-form clauses: *Central Inland Water Transport v Brojo Nath Ganguly* (1986); *Pioneer Urban Land v Govindan Raghavan* (2019, builder-buyer) [unverified-recall].
- **s.27**: an agreement restraining anyone from a lawful profession, trade or business is void to that extent. The only statutory exception is sale of goodwill, and Indian courts apply no English-style reasonableness test; post-term non-competes are generally void, restrictions during the term generally not [snippet: https://blog.ipleaders.in/section-27-of-indian-contract-act-1872/].
- **s.28** (amended 1997): clauses that restrict a party's legal remedies or shorten the time to sue are void, with exceptions for arbitration and (since 2013) bank guarantees [unverified-recall; *IndusInd Bank v Chandrakala Menon* (2016) on the amendment, snippet].
- **ss.73-74**: compensation is for loss that arises naturally, not remote or indirect loss (s.73). A named sum or penalty (s.74) gives only reasonable compensation up to that sum; *Kailash Nath Associates v DDA* (2015) 4 SCC 136 treats loss as a precondition, and commentators disagree on whether actual loss must be proved [snippet: https://indiacorplaw.in/2015/06/24/the-supreme-court-on-penalties-and/ ; https://www.barandbench.com/law-firms/view-point/disentangling-section-74-from-the-illusion-of-actual-loss-the-pitfalls-of-headnote-based-misinterpretation]. Relevance: an exit fee or lock-in penalty is not enforceable just because it is stated, but Redline cannot claim that it is unenforceable.
- **ss.126-147 (guarantee)**: the surety's liability matches the principal debtor's (s.128); a variation without the surety's consent discharges the surety (s.133) [unverified-recall]. This is relevant to the personal-guarantee Default Red line, and to "as amended from time to time" clauses alongside a guarantee.
- **Specific Relief Act 1963 as amended 2018** (in force 1 Oct 2018): specific performance is now the rule (s.10), not discretionary; substituted performance (s.20); contracts that cannot be specifically enforced include determinable contracts (s.14) [snippet: https://www.mondaq.com/india/contracts-and-commercial-law/737906/ ; https://ssrana.in/articles/india-the-specific-relief-amendment-act-2018/]. Relevance: the Owner may be made to perform a long lock-in rather than just pay damages. This raises the stakes of the auto-renewal or minimum-term Default Red line.

## Options (no decision recorded)
1. **Statute-first for India.** Where a floor exists (MSMED 45 days, s.27, s.74, registration over 1 year, state deposit caps), Redline's judgment says "departs from [statute, section]". This is the firmest ground but makes legal claims, which needs the owner's scope call (as in ticket 18 §D).
2. **Government forms as the balanced baseline.** Use GeM GTC for vendor and services terms (1x cap, 10-day pay, LD 0.5%/week to 5-10%) and the Maharashtra IGR format for leave and licence (1-month mutual notice). Primary, dated and quotable, but procurement- or residential-shaped.
3. **Curated India reference table.** Rows such as lock-in 3 years, escalation 15%/3 years or 5%/year, deposit 3-10 months, each with source URL, date and marking (deal report or blog), shown with the flag. Auditable, but the lease rows rest on snippets and need upkeep.
4. **Reuse the US forms as a labelled fallback.** For SaaS, cite Common Paper/Bonterms and label them US forms. Honest, but weak as an India "typical".
5. **Narrow Market standard claims in India** to clause types with a source (vendor payment terms, liability caps, deposits); leave the rest to Red lines with no comparison.

Open points for the owner: whether statute-based judgments are in scope; whether government procurement terms can stand for private B2B "Market standard"; whether Redline asks which state a lease is in (stamp duty, registration and tenancy caps differ by state); whether it needs to know the Owner's Udyam status (MSMED applies only to micro and small suppliers); and whether paid consultant data (JLL, CBRE and others) is worth licensing.
