# 18. Public sources that could ground "Market standard"

Researched 2026-10-31. Question: which public, citable sources describe typical or recommended terms for vendor agreements, commercial leases and client agreements in a US small-business setting, so that a Redline's judgment about an Unusual clause (ADR 0003) can point at something outside the model.

How sources are marked: **[fetched]** means I read the page. **[snippet]** means I saw only a search-result summary, so the claim is unverified.

## Summary

- **Vendor/SaaS agreements** have strong free sources: Common Paper and Bonterms standard forms (CC BY 4.0), plus Common Paper's open benchmark report, which gives actual percentages for liability caps, auto-renewal, notice windows and price increases.
- **Commercial leases** have no free quantitative survey. There are model leases (a Boston one from 2025) and small-business guidance (SBA, SBDC), but those are recommendations, not measurements of what is typical. Two statutes (CA SB 1103, NY GOL 5-903) set legal floors, which is a different kind of claim.
- **Client agreements** (the small business as the seller) are the thinnest area. The Common Paper/Bonterms Professional Services forms and the NY freelancer model contracts are the closest fits.
- **Clause datasets** (CUAD, LEDGAR) are licensed for reuse but built from public-company SEC filings, so they show what big-company contracts contain, not what small-business deals contain.

## A. Standard forms (what a balanced contract says)

### A1. Common Paper standard agreements [fetched]
- URL: https://commonpaper.com/standards/ ; Cloud Service Agreement v3.0: https://commonpaper.com/standards/cloud-service-agreement/
- Covers: Cloud Service Agreement, SLA, Professional Services Agreement, Software License, Order Form, SOW, Independent Contractor Agreement, NDA and others. Structure: fixed Standard Terms plus a Cover Page where the parties fill in the variables.
- Clause types (CSA v3.0): liability cap as a multiple of fees paid or payable in the prior 12 months (the multiplier is left blank); IP indemnity both ways; auto-renewal with a blank non-renewal notice date and an opt-out; termination for material breach after a 30-day cure, with no termination for convenience; optional renewal price increase by a set percentage, with or without notice.
- Jurisdiction: US-oriented; governing law is a cover-page variable.
- Licence: CC BY 4.0, per the standards page. No publication date is shown; the CSA page says v3.0.
- Limits: written for B2B SaaS. A "Market standard" claim for a vendor SaaS contract fits; for a lease it does not.
- How to cite: "Common Paper Cloud Service Agreement v3.0, §[n], CC BY 4.0", linking the versioned URL. Older versions have stable paths (for example `/standards/cloud-service-agreement/1.0/...` [snippet]).

### A2. Bonterms Standard Agreements [fetched home page; details from snippets]
- URL: https://bonterms.com/ ; committee: https://bonterms.com/committee [snippet]
- Covers: Cloud Terms, Software License Terms, Professional Services Agreement, NDA, DPA/BAA, Climate Addendum [fetched list].
- Drafted by a standing committee of 120+ in-house and law-firm lawyers, set up in 2021 [snippet]. Licence CC BY 4.0 "unless otherwise noted" [snippet].
- Version: the Cloud Terms are widely cited as V1.0 [snippet]. A "Committee Draft 4.0" cover page also exists [snippet]. Check the current version before citing.
- Clause types: the cap, indemnity, renewal and termination text was not verified here. The fetched page lists none of it.
- How to cite: "Bonterms Cloud Terms v[x], §[n], CC BY 4.0". The direct form URL I tried returned 404, so the URL needs confirming.

## B. Measured benchmarks (what deals actually contain)

### B1. Common Paper 2026 SaaS Contract Benchmark Report [fetched]
- URL: https://commonpaper.com/resources/2026-saas-contract-benchmark-report/ (earlier edition: https://commonpaper.com/resources/contract-benchmark-2024-q1/ [snippet])
- Method: 16,140 signed agreements from 2,223 companies on the Common Paper platform, June 2025 to June 2026, compared with the prior year. Covers CSAs, NDAs and Design Partner Agreements. Customer size is not stated.
- Figures (2026): 99% of CSAs use a multiplier cap, and 96% of those cap at 1x; unlimited-liability claims rose from 1.1% to 10.2%; supercaps 6.9%; indemnity covered claims in 85-90%; auto-renewal 87%; a 30-day non-renewal notice in about 70%; automatic fee increases fell from 23% (2024) to 13%; invoice payment is net 30.
- Caveats: the sample is self-selected (only companies that use Common Paper's forms), and those forms are balanced, so the figures lean toward balance. The report states no licence for its own figures; citing a fact with attribution is probably fine, but copying the report is a separate question. No termination figures.
- How to cite: "Common Paper, 2026 SaaS Contract Benchmark Report: 87% of cloud service agreements auto-renew."

### B2. World Commerce & Contracting, Most Negotiated Terms [snippet only]
- Annual report ranking the most-negotiated terms. Limitation of liability and indemnity have been the top two for years, followed by price/payment and termination. It ranks what gets negotiated; it says nothing about typical values. The report is largely member-gated. Little use for "typical", but it could support a claim like "this term is commonly negotiated".
- URL: https://www.worldcc.com/ (no public report URL found).

### B3. TermScout [fetched blog]
- A commercial benchmarking database (it claims 750+ data points per contract). It publishes no public figures and states no licence. Treat as a paid vendor, not a citable source.

### B4. Commercial lease surveys [snippet]
- No free national survey of small-business lease terms was found. One local survey (Los Alamos MainStreet, 2021, 18 tenants: 69% had rent escalation clauses) is too small to use. Trade-press figures like "3% annual escalation is typical" are uncited. A LeaseLens page gives averages with no method; do not use it. Broker datasets (CoStar and the like) are paid and not reusable.

## C. Guidance and model documents for small businesses

### C1. SBA [snippet]
- An archived SBA article, "Leasing Commercial Space" (2012), is reported in a search snippet to recommend one- to two-year leases with a renewal option for small businesses. I could not load the archived page, so this is unverified. Archive URL: https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/web/20121002061313/http://www.sba.gov/content/leasing-commercial-space
- The current sba.gov offers lease-negotiation workshops (event pages, for example https://www.sba.gov/event/85573), not a citable written standard. The Business Guide URL I tried returned 404.
- Value: US government guidance (public domain), but it is advice, not a measurement, and the one concrete claim is old.

### C2. LA Regional SBDC commercial leasing checklist workshop [fetched]
- https://smallbizla.org/workshop/commercial-leasing-checklist-for-businesses-16/ . A 2022 webinar page with no downloadable checklist. It warns that "standard" form leases are negotiable. Not usable as a source of terms.

### C3. Lawyers for Civil Rights BizGrow Model Commercial Lease (2025) [fetched page; PDF not read]
- Page: https://lawyersforcivilrights.org/our-impact/economic-justice/new-resource-for-small-businesses-model-lease/
- Lease: https://lawyersforcivilrights.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/LCR-BizGrow-Model-Commercial-Lease-2025.pdf ; User Guide in the same folder.
- With Anderson & Kreiger LLP. Retail use in Class A buildings, Boston area (Massachusetts). Plain language, with "standard and necessary landlord protections" kept. The page states no licence, so reuse needs permission. Term, renewal, guarantee and escalation text not yet checked: read the PDF next.
- The only small-business-tenant model lease found. It is local and a recommendation, not a measurement.

### C4. ABA, The Commercial Lease Formbook (2nd ed., c2010) [snippet]
- https://www.americanbar.org/products/inv/book/215323/ . A paid book covering landlord and tenant views. Citable as a reference, but neither free nor current.

### C5. GSA lease forms (federal) [snippet]
- GSA Form 3517A (General Clauses, small/simplified leases) and 3517B (larger leases), revised several times; for example https://www.gsa.gov/cdnstatic/GSA3517B-16.pdf . Public domain. The federal government is the tenant and dictates the terms, so these are not market terms for a small private tenant. Weak fit.

### C6. NYC and NY State freelancer model contracts [fetched NYC page]
- NYC DCWP Freelance Isn't Free Act page links a "Model Contract for Freelance Workers": https://www.nyc.gov/site/dca/businesses/freelance-isnt-free-act.page (FAQ dated 2018). NY State DOL has a statewide model under GBL Art. 44-A, in force 2024-08-28: https://dol.ny.gov/node/54011 [snippet].
- Covers individual freelancer and client contracts (written contract, timely full payment), not B2B. Government work, so likely free to cite. Relevant to client agreements only where the user is a sole freelancer.

## D. Statutes (legal floors, not market practice)

A statute is a different kind of claim from "typical": it can make a clause unenforceable or require notice. These are worth knowing about, but using them would mean Redline making legal claims, which is a scope question.
- **NY GOL § 5-903** [fetched]: https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GOB/5-903 . For service, maintenance or repair contracts (B2B included) that auto-renew for more than one month, the provider must send written notice of the renewal clause 15-30 days before the cancel-by date, or the renewal is unenforceable against the recipient.
- **California SB 1103 (Commercial Tenant Protection Act)**, effective 2025-01-01 [snippet; law-firm summaries such as https://www.gtlaw.com/en/insights/2025/1/new-statute-affects-small-business-leases-in-california]. For "qualified commercial tenants" (microenterprises, small restaurants, small nonprofits; the thresholds differ between summaries), it lengthens notice for rent increases and termination and limits operating-cost pass-throughs. Check the enacted text (Civ. Code §§ 827, 1946.1, 1950.9).

## E. Clause datasets

### E1. CUAD v1 (Atticus Project) [fetched]
- https://www.atticusprojectai.org/cuad ; categories: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/TheAtticusProject/cuad/main/category_descriptions.csv ; card: https://huggingface.co/datasets/theatticusproject/cuad-qa
- 510 contracts from SEC EDGAR, 25 types (Service, Supply, Maintenance, Hosting, Reseller, Outsourcing, Consulting and others; no leases), 13,000+ expert labels across 41 categories. The relevant categories are Renewal Term, Notice Period to Terminate Renewal, Termination for Convenience, Price Restrictions, Minimum Commitment, Cap on Liability, Uncapped Liability, Liquidated Damages, Insurance and Anti-Assignment. No personal guarantee or indemnity category.
- Licence CC BY 4.0, commercial use allowed. The authors give no warranty on the underlying contracts.
- Limits: these are public-company material contracts (NeurIPS 2021), not small-business deals. The labels mark whether a clause is present; they do not record values (cap amount, notice days), so "typical" values would have to be extracted again.

### E2. LEDGAR (in LexGLUE) [snippet]
- https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.00976 . About 850k provisions from EDGAR filings, labelled by topic (12.5k labels; LexGLUE uses 80k provisions and 100 labels). LexGLUE is tagged CC BY 4.0. The labels give topic only, not values or favourability, and the data comes from public companies. Useful for finding a clause, not for saying what is typical.

## Options (no decision recorded)

1. **Per contract type, cite a named standard form.** Vendor/SaaS: Common Paper CSA v3.0 or Bonterms Cloud Terms. Client services: Common Paper/Bonterms PSA. Lease: the LCR model lease (Boston, licence unclear). The label would read "departs from [form, §]". This is traceable, but a balanced form is a recommendation, not "typical".
2. **Cite measured figures where they exist.** Use the Common Paper benchmark for SaaS caps, renewal, notice and price increases, and fall back to option 1 or to no comparison elsewhere. Strongest for vendor SaaS, missing for leases.
3. **Build a curated reference table.** Redline keeps a versioned table of clause type, typical position and source URL, and shows the row it used. This makes every judgment auditable, but someone has to maintain it, and lease rows would rest on thin sources.
4. **Extract statistics from CUAD/LEDGAR.** Licensed for reuse, but the public-company bias and the lack of values mean real work for a weak small-business signal.
5. **Narrow what "Market standard" claims.** Make unusualness claims only where a source exists (mainly vendor SaaS). Elsewhere, leave the clause to Red lines and "Know this" with no comparison. Fewer claims, all of them backed.

Open points for the owner: whether quoting statutes (section D) is in scope; licence permission for the LCR lease; whether quoting Common Paper's benchmark figures needs permission; and whether a recommendation-type source can be labelled as "Market standard" at all (CONTEXT.md defines it as what a Counterparty would *typically offer*).
