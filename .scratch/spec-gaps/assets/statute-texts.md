## 1. MSMED Act 2006 (Act 27 of 2006): s.2(b), s.15, s.16 (row C2)

**Status: VERIFIED (non-consolidated). This is the Gazette text as enacted.**

- **Source:** Gazette of India Extraordinary, Part II, Section 1, No. 31, New Delhi, Friday, June 16, 2006 (Ministry of Law and Justice, Legislative Department), a scan hosted by the Department of Industries, Government of NCT of Delhi:

  s.2 is on Gazette page 2 and ss.15–16 are on page 8. Both were transcribed from the rendered page images; the OCR text layer has errors such as "d2Y".
- **Also fetched:** the MSME Ministry's Samadhaan copy, https://samadhaan.msme.gov.in/WriteReadData/DocumentFile/MSMED2006act.pdf. It is image-only, 11 pages, and was not transcribed.
- **Date read:** 2026-10-31
- **Amendments:** not confirmed. India Code's consolidated text was blocked (403). No amendment to ss.2(b), 15 or 16 was seen in any source read, but that has not been checked against a consolidated official text.

### s.2(b) (marginal note to s.2: "Definitions.")

> 2. In this Act, unless the context otherwise requires,—
>
> (b) "appointed day" means the day following immediately after the expiry of the period of fifteen days from the day of acceptance or the day of deemed acceptance of any goods or any services by a buyer from a supplier.
>
> *Explanation*.—For the purposes of this clause,—
>
> (i) "the day of acceptance" means,—
>
> (a) the day of the actual delivery of goods or the rendering of services; or
>
> (b) where any objection is made in writing by the buyer regarding acceptance of goods or services within fifteen days from the day of the delivery of goods or the rendering of services, the day on which such objection is removed by the supplier;
>
> (ii) "the day of deemed acceptance" means, where no objection is made in writing by the buyer regarding acceptance of goods or services within fifteen days from the day of the delivery of goods or the rendering of services, the day of the actual delivery of goods or the rendering of services;

This is relevant to the "Udyam-registered" wording in C2. s.2(n) as enacted (Gazette pages 2–3, from the text layer):

> (n) "supplier" means a micro or small enterprise, which has filed a memorandum with the authority referred to in sub-section (1) of section 8, and includes,— (i) the National Small Industries Corporation, being a company, registered under the Companies Act, 1956; (ii) the Small Industries Development Corporation of a State or a Union territory, by whatever name called, being a company registered under the Companies Act, 1956; (iii) any company, co-operative society, trust or a body, by whatever name called, registered or constituted under any law for the time being in force and engaged in selling goods produced by micro or small enterprises and rendering services which are provided by such enterprises;

### s.15 (marginal note: "Liability of buyer to make payment.")

Chapter V heading: DELAYED PAYMENTS TO MICRO AND SMALL ENTERPRISES

> 15. Where any supplier supplies any goods or renders any services to any buyer, the buyer shall make payment therefor on or before the date agreed upon between him and the supplier in writing or, where there is no agreement in this behalf, before the appointed day:
>
> Provided that in no case the period agreed upon between the supplier and the buyer in writing shall exceed forty-five days from the day of acceptance or the day of deemed acceptance.

### s.16 (marginal note: "Date from which and rate at which interest is payable.")

> 16. Where any buyer fails to make payment of the amount to the supplier, as required under section 15, the buyer shall, notwithstanding anything contained in any agreement between the buyer and the supplier or in any law for the time being in force, be liable to pay compound interest with monthly rests to the supplier on that amount from the appointed day or, as the case may be, from the date immediately following the date agreed upon, at three times of the bank rate notified by the Reserve Bank.

### Differences from draft row C2

- **"No more than 45 days after acceptance":** the statute says "from the day of acceptance or the day of deemed acceptance". Both terms are defined. Acceptance is normally the day of actual delivery or rendering of services. If the buyer objects in writing within 15 days, it is the day the supplier removes the objection, so it can fall later than delivery. The cap applies to the period "agreed upon … in writing".
- **"15 days with no written agreement":** the statute says payment is due "before the appointed day". The appointed day is the day after 15 days from acceptance or deemed acceptance. So payment is due within 15 days of acceptance, not of delivery or invoice.
- **"compound interest at 3x the RBI bank rate":** the statute says "compound interest with monthly rests … at three times of the bank rate notified by the Reserve Bank". The draft leaves out "with monthly rests". Interest runs from the appointed day or from the day after the agreed date.
- **"whatever the contract says":** the statute is wider: "notwithstanding anything contained in any agreement between the buyer and the supplier or in any law for the time being in force".
- **"Udyam-registered":** this is not statute text. As enacted, s.2(n) defines "supplier" as a micro or small enterprise "which has filed a memorandum with the authority referred to in sub-section (1) of section 8". It also includes NSIC, state SIDCs, and bodies selling MSE goods or services. Udyam does not appear in the enacted text. Later amendments to s.7 or s.8 (and any notifications under them) were not checked.

## 2. Indian Contract Act 1872 (Act 9 of 1872): s.27 with Exception 1 (row C4)

**Status: VERIFIED on a state government host, but the copy is retyped. India Code's exact typography was not confirmed.**

- **Source:** Commercial Tax Department, Government of Uttar Pradesh, "The Indian Contract Act, 1872 (Act no. 9 of 1872)", 45 pages, text-layer PDF:
  https://comtax.up.nic.in/Miscellaneous%20Act/the-indian-contract-act-1872.pdf
  s.27 is on PDF pages 16–17.
- **Date read:** 2026-10-31
- **Caveat:** this is a retyped compilation, not a Gazette scan or the India Code PDF. It has obvious typos in the neighbouring sections, for example "Agreements in restrain of legal proceedings" and "void to the extent" in s.28. Its spelling and capitalisation in s.27 may therefore differ from the India Code text: "anyone" vs "any one", "goodwill" vs "good-will", "court" vs "Court". The India Code PDF (https://indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/2187/2/A187209.pdf) returned 403. Search snippets of its arrangement of sections show the heading as "Saving of agreement not to carry on business of which good-will is sold". Footnotes, if any, were not seen.

### s.27 as published on comtax.up.nic.in (verbatim)

> 27. Agreement in restraint of trade, void
> Every agreement by which anyone is restrained from exercising a lawful profession, trade or business of any kind, is to that extent void.
>
> Exception 1 : Saving of agreement not to carry on business of which good will is sold
> - One who sells the goodwill of a business may agree with the buyer to refrain from carrying on a similar business, within specified local limits, so long as the buyer, or any person deriving title to the goodwill from him, carries on a like business therein, provided that such limits appear to the court reasonable, regard being had to the nature of the business.

**Corroboration (official, but a paraphrase, not the bare text):** Supreme Court of India, *Vijaya Bank v. Prashant B Narnaware*, 2025 INSC 691 (14 May 2025), para 11, https://api.sci.gov.in/supremecourt/2014/40179/40179_2014_9_1503_61700_Judgement_14-May-2025.pdf:

> "Section 27 of the Contract Act provides every agreement which restrains a person from exercising a lawful profession, trade or business of any kind is to that extent void. A sole exception is carved out in the proviso with regard to sale of goodwill of a business, in which case the seller may be restrained from carrying on similar business within a reasonable local limit."

### Differences from draft row C4

- **"restrains a lawful trade":** the statute says "a lawful profession, trade or business of any kind". The draft narrows this to trade only.
- **"only exception: sale of goodwill":** the bare text numbers it "Exception 1" and shows no other exception. The Supreme Court in 2025 calls it the "sole exception", so "only exception" is supported as a description. The exception is also conditional, which the draft leaves out:
  - the restraint covers carrying on "a similar business";
  - it must be "within specified local limits";
  - it lasts only "so long as the buyer, or any person deriving title to the goodwill from him, carries on a like business therein";
  - the limits must "appear to the court reasonable, regard being had to the nature of the business".
- **"Void to the extent it restrains":** this matches "is to that extent void".

### Differences from draft row L7

- **"A lease over one year must be registered":** s.17(1)(d) covers three kinds of lease: "from year to year, or for any term exceeding one year, or reserving a yearly rent". The draft names only the second.
- **"unregistered, it cannot prove the transaction":** this overstates s.49.
  - The section has three effects. An unregistered required document shall not (a) affect the immovable property, (b) confer any power to adopt (central wording UNVERIFIED, FAOLEX only; UP: "confer any power or create any right or relationship"), or (c) be received as evidence of any transaction affecting the property, "unless it has been registered".
  - The proviso then lets the document be received as evidence of a contract in a suit for specific performance (central text, UNVERIFIED wording). It also lets it be received as evidence of "any collateral transaction not required to be effected by registered instrument". The collateral-transaction limb appears in both the UP and FAOLEX texts.
- **Part performance:** since Act 48 of 2001 (VERIFIED), the central proviso no longer allows an unregistered document as evidence of part performance under s.53A TPA. The UP text still contains that limb but omits the specific-performance words. If a counter-offer or explanation relies on the proviso, the wording depends on the state.
- **State amendments:** at least UP (UP Act 57 of 1976, per the UP copy's notes) has amended ss.17 and 49; other states were not checked. The draft row cites the central Act only.
